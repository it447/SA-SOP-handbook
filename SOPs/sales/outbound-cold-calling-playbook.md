## Core rule

Keep the call conversational. Use scripts as prompts, not a speech — ask only what's needed to determine fit and move to the next step. Do not proactively bring up pricing; discuss it only when the prospect asks.

## Who this is for

Sales Team, Sales Managers, Outbound team.

## Playbook overview

This combines the cold-call talk track with the meeting-booked follow-up sequence. There are two entry paths: one for companies with a known open role, one where the hiring need is unknown.

**Quick path:** Open → Qualify → Explain → Route (not interested / curious / fully interested / send info) → Close (offer two meeting times, confirm email/time zone) → Execute the correct post-booking sequence.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Friendly, quick, casual — like catching them in the hallway.
- High energy, but not telemarketer energy. Smiling while talking changes your tone.
- Don't sound like a job applicant.
- Don't overpitch — one concise explanation, then a relevant question.
- Don't proactively mention pricing.
- Use one rebuttal maximum; if they decline again, end the call politely.
- Respect opt-outs immediately — record do-not-call requests and end the call.

## Tools & pre-call setup

Tools: a parallel dialer, HubSpot (CRM), Google Calendar, a tracking spreadsheet, and a HubSpot sequence that feeds leads into the dialer.

Setup steps: duplicate and rename the sequence and tracking sheet (one-time), get the lead list from the ops lead, enroll it into the duplicated HubSpot sequence, open the dialer and parallel-dial 5 leads at a time, and log the correct disposition after every call.

## Scenario One: job role present

Use when the company is actively hiring for a role confirmed in the lead data.

**Step 1 — Opening:** Introduce yourself and Scale Army, confirm the company, then reference the known open role: "I noticed you're hiring for [Role]. Is that role still open?" Keep it quick and observational, not like you're reading the job post.

**Step 2 — Hiring discovery:** Ask one question at a time about search stage (early vs. active interviews vs. offer stage), experience level needed, openness to a full-time remote hire aligned to their working hours, and whether the contact is the decision-maker.

**Step 3 — Brief explanation:** "That's exactly where we can help — we recruit and onboard vetted, full-time remote [function] talent. Would it be useful to compare that option with the search you're running now?" Keep it short unless they ask for more.

**If the role is no longer open:**
- **Filled:** ask if anything similar is likely to come up in the next few months.
- **Paused:** ask whether it's timing, budget, or a priority shift; if they expect it to restart, lock a specific follow-up date.
- **Using an agency:** ask if they're fully satisfied or open to comparing if the search stalls (one rebuttal only).
- **Already interviewing:** ask if they're happy with candidate quality or still have gaps.
- **Wrong person:** ask who's overseeing that search and request a name/transfer/contact info without pressuring the gatekeeper.

## Scenario Two: job role not present

Use when there's no confirmed hiring signal in the lead data.

**Step 1 — Opening:** State the business reason clearly so it doesn't read as a job application: "We help companies recruit and onboard vetted, full-time remote talent. I wanted to see whether hiring is on your radar now or in the next few months."

**Step 2 — Identify the hiring need:** Ask which part of the team is most likely to grow (sales/marketing, ops/support, or technical), experience level, timing, remote openness, and decision involvement. Offer no more than 2–3 example roles at a time.

**Step 3 — Brief explanation:** State the functions Scale Army recruits across (sales, marketing, customer support, operations, admin, finance, technical), then ask if it's worth exploring for the role they mentioned.

**If they're not hiring:** ask about future timing; if it may come up later, offer to send a short overview; if there's no plan at all, thank them and end the call politely; if they handle hiring fully in-house, ask whether they ever use an outside recruiting partner when new roles come up.

## Interest-level flows (both scenarios)

**Not interested:** one brief rebuttal clarifying whether it's the hiring need or the remote-talent model that's the blocker, then end the call if they decline again. Never call back repeatedly in the same session — log the disposition and move on.

**Semi-interested / curious:** give the one-line explanation, then ask what role would be most useful for their team — don't turn the answer into a long pitch.

**Fully interested:** before booking, confirm the role, experience level, timing/search stage, openness to full-time remote offshore talent, and whether the prospect is the decision-maker. Transition: "the next step is a short call with one of our account executives to go deeper on the role and the process" — see the [[sales-training-ae-onboarding-sop|AE Onboarding SOP]] for how that call is run.

## Common objections

- **"Not a priority":** ask what would need to change for it to become one.
- **"Too busy":** offer a short call instead and give two time options.
- **"Bad experience with remote employees":** ask whether it was quality, communication, management, or time-zone alignment.
- **"No budget":** ask whether it's timing or the expected cost of the role.
- **"Take me off your list":** confirm immediately, add to the do-not-call list, and end the call.

## Common questions and approved answers

- **Cost:** roles start around $2,000/month; mid-to-senior roles generally start around $3,000/month, with exact pricing depending on role/experience/market — the AE gives an accurate range. Never bring pricing up proactively.
- **Candidate locations:** recruiting spans ~14 countries, matched to role, working hours, and talent market.
- **Process:** understand the role and requirements → run a targeted search → vet candidates → support onboarding.
- **Speed:** qualified candidates typically within 14 days.
- **Existing candidate pool:** a fresh search is generally run per role to match specific needs.
- **Commitment:** month-to-month contracts, no long-term lock-in.
- **How remote hires integrate:** they work as part of the client's team using the client's systems, tools, hours, and performance expectations.
- **If a hire doesn't work out:** a replacement is provided quickly to minimize disruption.
- **Functions covered:** sales, marketing, customer support, operations, admin, finance, engineering, and other remote-compatible technical roles.
- **Vs. an agency/freelancer platform:** the focus is recruiting vetted full-time talent for the specific role plus onboarding support, not a general marketplace.
- **"Can you send info first?"** confirm the best email and role to tailor it to, then set a specific reconnect date/time.
- **"How did you get my number?"** business contact sourcing for outreach; apologize if it's a bad time and honor any opt-out request immediately.

## Booking the meeting

Offer two specific time options with time zone; confirm email, date, time, attendees, and what the call will cover before hanging up. If they need to check their calendar, offer a tentative hold with a confirm-by time. If they want another decision-maker included, get that person's name/email for the invite. If they want information first, send it and set a specific follow-up.

**Tonality cheat sheet:** opening — quick/relaxed/professional; known role — observant, not intrusive; unknown need — curious, neutral; company explanation — short, credible, followed by a question; pricing — calm, matter-of-fact; objections — patient, non-defensive; booking — smooth, assumptive; unqualified/hostile calls — polite, flat, fast.

## Follow-up

**Immediately after every call:** log the correct disposition, apply do-not-call status if requested, and leave notes another rep could pick up without needing to replay the call.

**Immediately after booking:** send the meeting-confirmation email and initial booking text; a few hours later send a "quick heads-up" email with approved case studies; the day before and day of, send reminder emails (and a day-of text only if automation hasn't already covered it).

**"Send me information" follow-up:** confirm email and relevant role, send only approved materials/proof, and lock a specific follow-up date/time/time zone.

**Email/text templates:** tailor the meeting-confirmation email to whether the company is D2C or B2B (use the neutral version if unclear), referencing only approved case studies and testimonials. Any testimonial or proof point must be verified and approved before use — do not send an unverified quote or claim.
