## Goal

A standardized framework for financial forecasting and budgeting — critical given the company's flat-monthly-fee business model rather than hourly billing. Covers annual/quarterly budgeting (setting targets) and forecasting (predicting performance from current data/trends).

## Who this is for

Finance Team, Department Heads, and Upper Management.

## Key definitions

- **Active Contractors (External):** client-facing resources currently placed and generating revenue via flat monthly fees.
- **Active Contractors (Internal):** internal contractors across all departments.
- **Base Revenue:** revenue from current active external contractors at their contracted monthly rates.
- **COGS:** direct costs tied to external contractors (their monthly rates).
- **Base Expenses:** calculated expenses from internal contractor rates.
- **Rebudget:** a quarterly review/adjustment of the annual budget for current conditions.
- **P&L:** the primary statement showing revenue, COGS, expenses, and profitability.
- **Actuals:** historical financial data from closed months in the P&L.

## Budgeting process

**Annual budget:** late September–early October, ~4–6 weeks, produces the approved annual budget.
**Quarterly rebudget:** mid-month of the last month of each quarter, ~2–3 weeks, produces an adjusted quarterly budget.

### Phase 0 — Template prep (Week 0, Finance)
Create a new dated folder in the Budgets Drive (e.g. "2026 Q1") with a "Departments" subfolder; copy the Consolidated P&L template into the year folder and the Shared Services, Operations, Demand Marketing, and Sales departmental templates into the Departments subfolder, using the naming convention "[Number] - [Department] - [Version]". Update all year/quarter references and month labels, clear prior-year data while preserving formulas, relink all IMPORTRANGE connections between departmental templates and the main P&L, and verify formulas/subtotals/tab linkage before proceeding.

### Phase 1 — Revenue & COGS foundation (Week 1, Finance)
Pull the current list of active external contractors from All Sales (names, client names, client rates, contractor pay rates). Populate the Client Fees tab (base revenue) and Base Rate tab (base COGS) in the Operations template for each month — the template calculates monthly/yearly revenue, COGS, and margin. Document known contractor end dates, renewals due, and planned rate increases.

### Phase 2 — Internal expenses / personnel (Week 1–2, Finance + HR)
Pull the full internal contractor list by department (names, roles, monthly rates, department). Populate each department's Contractors tab (name, title, start/end date, monthly gross payments, bonuses) and confirm it flows into the USD tab. Apply compensation adjustment assumptions (collaborate with department heads/HR for the annual budget; only include already-approved adjustments for a quarterly rebudget). Calculate commission expenses by role (e.g. AEs at 5% of revenue) and populate the formula into the Contractors tab.

### Phase 3 — Operating expenses baseline (Week 2, Finance)
Pull all operating expenses from the most recently closed month, categorized by department and type (software, advertising, office, professional services, travel, other). Populate the Expenses tab per department for January, exclude one-time/non-recurring items, populate February–December, and flag seasonal variation using the Description column for department-head context.

### Phase 4 — Department collaboration (Week 2–3, Finance + Department Heads)
Send each department head their personnel/operating expense breakdown for review. Collect input on planned software changes, advertising spend shifts, headcount changes, one-time project expenses, and other material changes. Document and incorporate all requested changes, flagging significant variances for management review.

### Phase 5 — Revenue goal setting (Week 3–4, Finance + Upper Management)
Present the baseline scenario (current contractors, margins, gap to target). Get required input from leadership: target revenue growth %, target new placements, target margin, and strategic priorities. Model conservative/moderate/aggressive scenarios against delivery capacity, then finalize the target scenario and document assumptions.

### Phase 6 — Final review & approval (Week 4–6, Finance + Upper Management)
Compile the full budget package (revenue, COGS, personnel with adjustments/commissions, opex by department, key KPIs). Present in a formal review, incorporate final requested changes, then obtain formal approval, lock the budget version, distribute to department heads, and set up monthly variance reporting.

### Phase 7 — Documentation & communication (final week, Finance)
Save the approved version with version control, document assumptions/methodology, create an executive summary, and configure budget-vs-actual reporting with a monthly review cadence and variance escalation thresholds.

## Financial forecasting process

**Timing:** on-demand, typically monthly (mid-month). **Duration:** 1–3 days depending on scope.

**Step 1 — Define scope (requestor):** clarify time horizon, components needed (revenue/COGS only, full P&L, or cash flow), specific scenarios, and urgency; document the request and confirm scope in writing.

**Step 2 — Work from the most recent actuals (Finance):** open the current year's P&L, identify the most recently closed month as the baseline (no need to rebuild base schedules from scratch), and verify the actuals are complete and error-free.

**Step 3 — Apply revenue & COGS adjustments (Finance):** use the linked variable/adjustment sheets — editable fields that auto-update future (non-actual) months in the P&L — to reflect expected new contractor starts, known terminations, rate changes, and pipeline probability. Sanity-check resulting gross margins.

**Step 4 — Adjust expenses if needed (Finance, with department input):** for simple revenue/COGS forecasts expenses may stay at budget; for comprehensive forecasts, update the expense variable sheets for new/canceled software, planned campaign spend, one-time expenses, and headcount changes, then confirm the changes flow correctly.

**Step 5 — Review & finalize (Finance):** calculate revenue growth vs. budget/prior year, gross/operating margin, projected profitability, and cash flow if requested. Run variance analysis against budget and the prior forecast, explaining material drivers. If requested, build Base/Upside/Downside scenarios using copies of the variable sheets so the base forecast isn't disturbed.

**Step 6 — Documentation & delivery (Finance):** prepare a summary dashboard, the P&L with actuals/forecast months clearly distinguished, documented assumptions, scenario comparisons, and risk/opportunity notes. Review internally, deliver in the agreed format, and archive the dated version with its assumptions for future accuracy tracking.

## Roles & responsibilities

- **Finance Team:** leads all budgeting/forecasting, maintains models/templates, manages IMPORTRANGE connections, coordinates department input, runs calculations/variance analysis, owns documentation/version control.
- **Department Heads:** provide expense input, communicate headcount/spending changes, review/approve their department's budget, give variance context.
- **HR:** provides headcount data, advises on compensation planning, confirms internal contractor rates.
- **Sales/Account Management:** provide pipeline intelligence, flag at-risk contracts/renewals, validate revenue assumptions.
- **Upper Management:** sets targets, approves budgets, requests ad-hoc forecasts, makes resource-allocation decisions.
- **Delivery/Operations:** confirm contractor capacity/feasibility and attrition risk, validate external contractor data.

## Tools

Google Sheets (all templates, P&L, department budgets, variable sheets), HubSpot/All Sales (contractor and pipeline data), the payroll system (contractor rates), and the accounting system (historical actuals). Key templates: Main P&L, Annual Budget, Quarterly Rebudget, Client Fees tab, Base Rate tab, departmental templates, forecast variable sheets, and the Budget vs. Actual variance report.

## Critical guidelines

- Always use the most current contractor data and verify assumptions with department heads.
- Always create fresh template copies per period — never edit prior-year templates directly — and re-verify IMPORTRANGE connections after copying.
- Document every assumption in writing and distinguish known facts from projections.
- Keep stakeholders informed of timeline/progress and escalate risks immediately.
- Use clear version numbering and date stamps; archive historical versions.
- Forecast from the most recent actuals using variable sheets for targeted changes rather than rebuilding base schedules.
- Compare forecasts to actuals over time to refine assumptions and improve accuracy.
