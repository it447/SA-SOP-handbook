## Overview

This is the Account Manager's full operating guide, covering the entire client journey from the [[glossary-account-managers|Kick-Off Call]] through closing the deal. Follow the steps in order.

**Steps Overview:**
- Step One: Getting Introduced + Kick-Off Call
- Step Two: Prepping for CRS Meeting
- Step Three: The Candidate Review Session (CRS)
- Step Four: The Interview Block
- Step Five: A Candidate Presentation Block
- Step Six: Closing the Deal
- Step Seven: Post-Onboarding Referral Ask

---

## Step One: Example Candidates & Kick-Off Call

### Why it matters

- A live Google Meet is far more impactful than a simple email intro.
- It builds trust and establishes rapport.
- It clarifies expectations by letting you ask direct questions upfront.

### Before the call

- Introduce yourself once the AE has sent the intro email, and schedule the Kick-Off Call (KOC). The call should be 15–20 minutes (extend if needed).
- Send Batch Zero to the client via email.
- Schedule the call within 2 days of the AE's introduction email.

### During the call

**All calls must be video recorded.** AMs lead the call, with the SCP (Senior Client Partner) playing a supporting role — showing face, spotting upsell opportunities, and asking questions the AM might miss. Non-negotiables for the call:

1. Introduce yourself and reassure the client the search is underway.
2. Introduce your SCP and explain their role.
3. Confirm the budget and ensure the JD is accurate.
4. Ask granular questions about the ideal profile and gather feedback on the example candidates.
5. Address any recruiter questions and clarify what the client values.
6. Set clear expectations for the search timeline and schedule the Candidate Review Session (CRS).

**Additional strategic questions to ask:**

1. *"Who else will be involved in this process, and how do decisions typically get made on your side?"* — Reveals stakeholders, decision-making style, and potential bottlenecks. Helps AM/CS know who to loop in.
2. *"Have you worked with offshore talent before? If so, what worked well — and what didn't?"* (or, if not: *"What are your biggest concerns about working with offshore talent?"*) — Surfaces past frustrations and positioning opportunities.
3. *"What does success look like for you in this engagement — beyond just hiring the right person?"* — Aligns the team on what the client actually values (speed, quality, cost, outcomes).
4. *"If this were to go really well over the next 3–6 months, what would that look like for your business?"* — Surfaces growth/expansion potential for Sales, Client Success, and capacity planning.

### After the call: adding your notes

- A KOC notes channel exists for this. Once the KOC is complete, go to the client's folder (auto-created when Launch Search Ops happens), open the KOC notes doc, and fill in notes using the template and Gemini notes.
- Post to the KOC Slack channel using the "KOC Notes Alert" format: Client, Role, @RecruiterName, Rescope info if applicable (old/new JD links), and a link to the KOC Notes doc. Remove any bullets that don't apply (mostly relevant for rescopes).

### How to nail it

- This is your one shot to start the search on the right foot.
- Don't overpromise — be realistic and transparent.
- Address budgeting, JD, or scoping issues upfront.
- Research the client thoroughly before the call.
- This is the most important call in the process.

## Step Two: Prepping for the CRS

The CRS should be scheduled 5–7 days after the KOC. Add the SCP to the meeting as optional.

### Follow-up & communication cadence

- Update the client every 2–3 days, even without major news.
- Communicate more frequently when applicant volume is low, you're waiting on recruiter output, or the TRS is still being built.
- Use a soft-launch strategy if CRS timelines need adjusting.

### Prep the TRS + review candidates

- Calculate margins using the IRS and the margin calculator.
- Send the TRS to the client at least 1 day before the CRS.
- Prep your push list: know the candidates inside and out, push for interviews with top picks, speak up if a candidate doesn't fit.
- Goal: 2–4 candidates booked for interviews. Non-negotiable: at least one candidate you can confidently present.

### Running the CRS (prep phase)

Goals: secure interviews, collect feedback, unblock indecision.

Before wrapping up: lock in an Interview Block for 2–4 candidates. If the client is unsure, understand why and push for your favorites. If no Interview Block is secured, push for client feedback so you can fix the problem. If the client hasn't reviewed candidates yet, review together live or give them time and follow up.

## Step Three: Running the Candidate Review Session (CRS)

CRS Goals: real-time feedback on candidates, secured interviews for 2–4 strong candidates, and clear direction for next steps.

### Prepare for the CRS

- Review the TRS and identify top picks.
- Coordinate with your recruiter on push priorities.
- Watch the CRS preparation training videos (see internal Drive links in the original doc).
- Goal: clarity on which candidates to push, aligned with the recruiter.

### Leading the CRS

- Kick off confidently: state the agenda and remind the client of the meeting's goals.
- Ask targeted questions: "What's your first impression?" / "Any standout strengths or concerns?" / "Would you interview this candidate?"
- Push for commitment: aim for 2–4 interviews scheduled by the end. If the client hasn't reviewed candidates, suggest a follow-up CRS. If they haven't read the TRS, summarize key candidates live and let them review later.

### After the call

- Post all feedback in the Pod Slack channel, CC-ing the recruiter and other relevant people. Post notes immediately covering: who's moving forward, what feedback was given, any new instructions/preferences, and — most importantly — deadlines (interview block timing, or the next deadline if the client passed on everyone). CC recruiters/sourcers. Early on, tag Zeina/your SCP on everything; later this isn't required.

### Booking the Interview Block

- Confirm interview interest during the CRS: "Can we lock in a time to interview [Candidate]?"
- Offer interview times at least 2 days out — the team needs that time to run its own internal interview and confirm candidate availability first.
- Create the interview block: title "[Client Name] x Scale Army – Interview Block", 30 minutes per candidate, with the meeting link removed so recruiters can adjust it later.
- After the call, send the calendar invite to the client, inviting the recruiter and relevant points of contact.
- The recruiter splits the block into individual candidate slots. Once candidates are added, delete the original block **without** sending a notification — the client should only see the final individual interview blocks.

## Step Four: The Interview Block

Your job here is to keep things smooth, professional, and on track — gathering live feedback while preventing issues.

### Prepping for the Interview Block

Once the recruiter flags candidates as ready (post-[[candidate-screening-sop|Disco Call]]):

- Send prep materials to the recruiter: a written prep doc and a short Loom video (see examples in original doc).
- Book the Interview Block if not already set during the CRS: propose 2–3 times, include the recruiter, remove the meeting location so the recruiter can add candidate links. If the client proposes an individual time, check availability but book it on your own calendar.
- Join the A/V Prep Call 5 minutes before each interview. Recruiters run these, but it's good practice to jump in early (especially for the first interview block) to make sure things are running smoothly. Recruiters confirm internet, lighting, and audio.

### During the Interview Block

- Introduce the interview, then go muted/camera-off: "Hey [Client Name], I'll introduce you to [Candidate Name] and be muted/camera-off during the interview. In the last 5 minutes, I'll join back for feedback."
- While muted, watch for late arrivals, tech issues, disruptive environments, and prevent circumvention. Keep interviews on schedule.

### Owning the last 5 minutes

- Rejoin the call for the final 3–5 minutes. Gently interrupt for time management if needed.
- Dismiss the candidate politely: "Thanks so much for your time. You're free to hop off now!"
- Ask for immediate feedback: "What were the green, red, or beige flags?" Take detailed notes on what stood out, who they liked best, and whether they're ready to move forward.

### Post-interview action items

- Post feedback (red/green flags, next steps) in the Pod channel and let the recruiter know the client's preference.
- Confirm next actions: ready to move forward, need a second round, or need a take-home task.
- Coordinate take-home assignments if needed — make sure the task makes sense and nothing is missing (ChatGPT can help draft/check).
- Reassure the client with an update and an ETA for the revised TRS if needed.

### Tips

- Mirror client language to show you're listening (e.g. "Sounds like Samantha nailed the ownership piece — let's prioritize that trait moving forward.")
- Gently remind them of ongoing support: "We're screening until the final decision — every bit of feedback helps refine the pool."
- Stay consistent and professional across every Interview Block — repetition builds trust.

## Step Five: The Candidate Presentation Block

Typically 15–30 minutes, up to 45 minutes for technical roles (e.g. a live coding session). Before the block, the candidate sends in their take-home so it can be added to the TRS.

### Take-Home Task SOP for AMs

**Before sending to recruiter/candidate:** review the take-home fully — confirm it makes sense, includes all links/assets, is clear, and suggest changes if quality needs improving.

**After candidate submission:** review the completed task before it goes to the client — check accuracy, completeness, polish, and recommend changes so it's error-free, on-brand, and client-ready. The AM owns the quality of every client-facing asset.

### Scheduling & conducting the presentation

- Schedule the block ~1 day after the candidate submits their take-home, so they can walk the client through their thinking. Clients respond well to seeing personality and rationale, and it opens space for Q&A.
- During the presentation, stay on the call muted/camera-off, ready to rejoin for feedback at the end. If the client doesn't want a live presentation, that's fine — just send the take-home for review, but gently recommend the live presentation where possible.

## Step Six: Closing the Deal

### Send the "Next Steps" email

Once the client says yes, send the Next Steps email outlining the final steps before the hire starts, adjusting the template for the client's details, payment, and PTO preferences, and tailoring language for first-time vs. repeat clients.

### Let the team know

- Announce the hire in the POD channel (e.g. "Candidate X is a GO for [Client Name]! Waiting on final start date confirmation — will send the LOI soon.")
- Once the start date is confirmed, send the Letter of Intent (LOI) via the Dropbox Sign template.

### Offer Call

- After the LOI is signed, the Recruiter schedules an Offer Call with the candidate.
- Update the HubSpot deal card and fill out the "Get the offer out" Slack form.
- Keep the client updated (e.g. "We're scheduling an offer call with [Candidate Name], and you'll be the first to know when it's locked in!")

### Sending the MSA/Addendum and IP Agreement

- After the Offer Letter is signed, send the IP Agreement for signing.
- Once signed, send the MSA (new client) or Addendum (existing client) via HelloSign. **You must sign it yourself first**, before it goes to the client.
- Notify the team once sent, and again once signed — you'll get a notification in the `#dropbox_sign-msa-status-update` Slack channel.
- Upload the signed document to Google Drive and add the link to HubSpot.

### The final step

- Move the deal card in HubSpot and complete the Closed Won form that appears (this auto-populates in `#onboarding-won-deals-msa-signed`). Leave at least 2 business days between this form and the start date.
- Behind the scenes: Sandra handles client billing; HR starts onboarding the candidate in Deel.
- Send the Client ↔ Candidate intro email 2–3 days before the start date, using the HubSpot template, tweaked as needed. **This must be a brand-new email thread — never a reply in the existing client chain.** Include the relevant HR point of contact and the assigned SCP.

### Schedule the Client Success onboarding call

Once everything above is complete, schedule the Client Success onboarding call and include your SCP. The ideal start date is before the candidate's actual start date.

During the call:
- Walk through the onboarding process and clarify timelines/next steps.
- Look for natural upsell opportunities.
- Reassure the client that you'll keep tabs to ensure a smooth transition.

---

## Step Seven: Post-Onboarding Referral Ask

Once a client is a happy client, the goal is to turn them into a source of new clients. Each AM has a monthly referral goal, and should proactively ask existing happy clients for referrals every month.

> 1 brilliant AM + 1 happy client = more clients and more revenue.
